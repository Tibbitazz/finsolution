## 2. Bandwidth premise (unchanged position)

REF-01 §7 records as `CONTESTED` that ANG's premise, human bandwidth (paper p. 1; lecture s23), is weak for a single personal investor. On that reading, agent value must come from judgement, not throughput. The lecture restates the premise without new evidence; the position stands.

Consequence: judgement is where return-based tests have the least power (§6.6, D9). So process and verifiability evidence (RQ-55) is the measurable part of agent value, alongside returns. This does not change P7.

## 4. Agentic learning as a first-class component (owner priority, 2026-10-07)

### 4.1 Reference design (newest authorial statement)
Lecture s36 (Oct 2026), combined with paper §6.2 (pp. 27–28):

**Layer 1: agent memory.** Each agent keeps its own decision traces: the call, the outcome, the reflection.

**Layer 2: skill evolution.** Reasoning is compared with realised outcomes; skills and code are rewritten and deployed.

**Loop:** Execute → Observe → Evaluate → Diagnose → Modify → Test → Promote or roll back.

**Paper §6.2 meta agent.** Rolling 3-year feedback on:
- regime accuracy;
- cross-sectional rank correlation of expected returns;
- signal hit rates;
- per-method prediction error by asset class and regime.

It may edit skills, prompts and code, with human approval above a materiality threshold, IPS files excluded.

ADR-0023 §8 already adopts "a learning loop with human approval above materiality" as baseline. The paper and lecture are adopted as the **structure**; BARREL/ApexOS product specifics are not.

### 4.2 Learning objects (candidates for the S4.3 taxonomy)

| Object | Content |
|---|---|
| Forecast Record | Every quantitative claim by any role (CMA, regime, signal direction, risk forecast), with horizon, timestamp, evidence IDs and run ID, so it can be scored later |
| Outcome Record | Realised value for each Forecast Record at its horizon (point-in-time) |
| Decision Trace | Exists as the Decision Record (ADR-0026 §3); linked to outcomes |
| Reflection | Agent-written diagnosis of a trace and outcome; text only, archived (R7) |
| Agent Memory State | Versioned store an agent may read. **Part of the run manifest** (04 §1 extension candidate) |
| Skill Version | Versioned skill/description/script set (05 §3) |
| Change Proposal | Proposed modification: target, rationale, evidence, materiality class |
| Replay / Shadow Test | Re-run on archived inputs (04 replay mode), then forward shadow runs |
| Promotion / Rollback Decision | Gate record with test evidence and approver (ADR-0026 §4.4) |

### 4.3 Capture from day one [AD]
The engine cannot learn from data it did not record. **S8 requirement candidate:** Forecast Records, Outcome Records and versioned memory exist from the first runnable slice, even while learning permissions remain closed (S17).

### 4.4 Learning signals and their power (`VERIFIED-DERIVATION`, D10)
- **Outcome-based signals are weak at strategic frequency.** With quarterly evaluation, a 3-year window gives 12 observations per asset class. The SE of a hit rate at p = 0.5 is √(0.25/12) = 0.144, so a 60% hit rate is 0.69 SE from chance.
- **Process-based signals (RQ-55) give learnable signal with power:** rule adherence, known-answer accuracy, seeded-fault detection, repeat-run stability, R8 citation completeness.
- **The loop uses both.** Outcome signals are pooled across assets and time, with explicit uncertainty, and are never used alone to rewrite a skill.

### 4.5 Promotion protocol (candidate for S17; structure now)
1. Every change is tested on archived inputs (deterministic replay, 04) and then shadow-run forward before promotion. This is the lecture's "every change is tested before it ships".
2. **Promotion is per role**, with a **diversity check**. Lecture s36 deploys skill changes "to every agent", which raises the correlated-error risk the lecture itself lists (s38). Global deployment needs a measured diversity impact (RQ-56).
3. Mandate changes are material and human-approved (ADR-0026 §4.4). Method-Library and contract changes always go through the gate (B-16, C-6).

### 4.6 RQ-22 split
The **design** of learning (objects, capture, signals, protocol) moves forward to S4.3/S4.20/S8. **Permissions and materiality thresholds** stay at S17/G17.

## 5. Correlated agent errors (owner request: research; → RQ-56)

### 5.1 Evidence

| Source | Status | Finding (scope) |
|---|---|---|
| Kim, Garg, Peng & Garg (2025), "Correlated Errors in Large Language Models", ICML 2025 (arXiv 2506.07962) | `VERIFIED-SOURCE` (abstract read 2026-10-07) | Across 350+ LLMs, models agree 60% of the time when both err (one leaderboard). Shared architecture and provider drive correlation, but "larger and more accurate models have highly correlated errors, even with distinct architectures and providers". Effects shown for LLM-as-judge and hiring |
| Kleinberg & Raghavan (2021), "Algorithmic Monoculture and Social Welfare", *PNAS* | `VERIFIED-SECONDARY` (search summary) | Convergence on one algorithm can lower aggregate decision quality even when it is individually more accurate, without needing shocks |
| Panickssery, Bowman & Feng (2024), "LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations", NeurIPS 2024 | `VERIFIED-SECONDARY` | Self-preference bias in LLM judges, linked to self-recognition |
| Liang et al. (2024), "Encouraging Divergent Thinking … through Multi-Agent Debate", EMNLP 2024 (arXiv 2305.19118) | `VERIFIED-SECONDARY` | "Degeneration of thought" in self-reflection; debate helps; the judge model's identity matters |
| Diversity-of-thought study (arXiv 2410.12853) vs "Demystifying Multi-Agent Debate" (arXiv 2601.19921) | `VERIFIED-SECONDARY` | Mixed: diverse models sometimes help, but heterogeneous mixes often fall between the worst and best homogeneous setups |
| Schoenegger, Tuminauskaite, Park & Tetlock (2024), "Wisdom of the Silicon Crowd", arXiv 2402.19379 | `VERIFIED-SECONDARY` | A 12-LLM ensemble is statistically indistinguishable from a 925-person crowd on 31 questions. **Candidate correct source** for ANG p. 3's crowd claim (ANG-12; checklist M-6) |

**Implication (`INFERENCE`).** ANG's main mitigation, heterogeneous foundation models (p. 25), is weaker than assumed: frontier models make correlated errors across providers. Its second mitigation, deterministic optimisers (p. 25), is the robust one. Correlation must therefore be **measured**, not assumed away.

### 5.2 Mitigation design (candidates; owner stage in brackets)

| # | Mitigation | Mechanism | Stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Keep numbers in code | R1/R8. Correlated errors then affect narrative and bounded choices, not computation | In force |
| M2 | Deterministic ballast | Every run also computes the R2 deterministic path. Agent decisions are logged with their divergence from it | S8/S12 |
| M3 | Independence before exposure | Independent first-round outputs, then barrier and simultaneous release (ANG p. 12; repo barrier pattern) | S12 |
| M4 | Judge ≠ author | The CIO/judge model differs from the proposer model where feasible. Peer review cites deterministic cards, not prose style (self-preference) | S8/S12 |
| M5 | Model heterogeneity as a tested option | ANG's robustness test (one model family per category). Its value is measured (RQ-56), not assumed | S7/S8 |
| M6 | Measure error correlation | On verifiable tasks (seeded faults, known answers): agreement-when-both-wrong across agents and models (the Kim et al. metric); correlation of CMA-judge deviations | S7/S8 |
| M7 | Protect diversity in learning | Per-role promotion with a diversity check (§4.5) | S17 |
| M8 | Dissent visible | Bottom flags, rejected methods and dissent in the S14 report; no consensus-only presentation | S14 |

## 6. Sandbox lessons that generalise (derivations in §9)

### 6.1 Fixed per-trade minima make trading a leg a decision → RQ-18, RQ-44, RQ-07; S4.20
- With commission max(r·x, m), the minimum binds below x = m/r (US/other Mini: 24,500 NOK; D1).
- Small accounts with many positions face large build and rebalancing costs (D2, D3).
- **[AD] candidates:**
  - the number of positions, and whether to trade a leg, are capital-dependent decisions;
  - X-13 (no size buckets) requires a continuous treatment;
  - S4.20 contracts declare whether a method supports minimum-position, lot or cardinality constraints, or needs an implementability step (S13e).

### 6.2 Price class → CB-17; RQ-18; S1 field gap
**Crossovers (`VERIFIED-DERIVATION`):** Normal becomes cheaper than Mini above 49,500 NOK per trade (US/other) and 52,667 NOK (Nordic).

**Nordnet's price list (retrieved 2026-10-07) states the Nordic figure itself (`VERIFIED-SOURCE`):**
- Normal is the default;
- Mini must be chosen under "Mine sider" and suits trades under 52,667 NOK;
- Bonus applies after more than 15 trades a month in Normal; VIP after more than 30 in Normal or Bonus;
- welcome pricing for the current and next month, then automatically Normal;
- a student class (under 25, Lånekassen-approved school);
- US/other rates confirmed: Mini 0.2%/49, Normal 0.1%/99, Bonus 0.09%/89, VIP 0.08%/79; new-customer and student rows; algorithmic-order surcharge 0.03%.

**S1 field gap:** `ACC.accounts` has no price-class attribute. Its addition goes through ADR-0015 schema evolution at S13a.

### 6.3 FX conversion flows → CB-18; RQ-18
- Without a currency account, Nordnet converts automatically at "0.25% ved kjøp og 0.25% ved salg" (price list, 2026-10-07, `VERIFIED-SOURCE`).
- A switch between two USD instruments therefore costs ≈ 0.5% of the amount switched, versus ≈ 0 with a USD currency account (`INFERENCE` from the stated per-side charges).
- The cost model must model conversion **flows**, not a per-trade percentage.
- Residual: netting or timing of simultaneous buy and sell conversions.

### 6.4 Numéraire changes portfolios → RQ-07; S4.20 field
**`VERIFIED-DERIVATION` (first order; all assets in one foreign currency):**
- r_NOK ≈ r_USD + s, so Σ_NOK = Σ_USD + 1aᵀ + a1ᵀ with a = c + (σ_s²/2)1 and cᵢ = Cov(rᵢ, s).
- GMV weights are unchanged **iff** c ∝ 1.
- Numerical check: identical when c is equal across assets; a max weight change of 0.61 when it is not.

**Candidates:**
- contracts declare the currency and hedging basis of μ/Σ inputs;
- CMA candidates are put on a common basis (currency, nominal/real, total/excess, horizon, arithmetic/geometric) before any [min, max] bound.

### 6.6 Evaluation → RQ-09, RQ-26, RQ-37
- **Trial budgets:** the expected maximum Sharpe of N independent null strategies over T years is ≈ E[max of N std normals]/√T. For N = 50 and T = 5 that is ≈ 1.01 (D7). This motivates pre-registered trial budgets (RQ-37).
- **Detecting an agent's Sharpe improvement** ΔSR at t = 2 needs T ≈ 2(1−ρ)(2/ΔSR)² years (leading term). For ΔSR = 0.04 that is 250, 50 or 5 years at ρ = 0.95, 0.99, 0.999 (D9). Statistical detectability and economic size trade off.
- **Capital grid:** costs are non-linear in capital, so results are reported over a pre-registered capital grid, never at one capital level.
- **Nested-universe robustness:** a broad universe has short common history (an 18-ETF set starts ≈ 2010 and misses 2008), so pre-register a long-history core subset.
- **Daily-bar simulation conventions** (candidates):
  - limit orders fill only if the next range trades through;
  - market orders at the next open + half-spread;
  - worst-case ordering within a bar;
  - close signal → next tradable price.

### 6.7 Implementation → RQ-53; S4.15b T5/T9
- **Candidate plan menu:** immediate · even spread over the window · close · staged · conditional with a hard deadline then catch-up. Fallback: even spread.
- **Two pre-declared controls:** immediate implementation (P3) and even spread.
- **Power:** detecting a 10 bp average improvement with 2.5-day average delay and 1% daily volatility needs ≈ 1,000 independent legs (D8), so Trader value is reported as a process measure with CIs.
- The stress-period discipline test (§3.5).
- The execution mode (RQ-19, manual vs API) bounds the menu's time granularity.
- An intraday VWAP benchmark needs intraday data. A multi-day typical-price proxy is possible but adds trials.

### 6.8 As-of gate → RQ-34 (S8 interface)
- Every agent tool or data call is answered as of the decision timestamp.
- **Test:** scrambling post-decision data must not change any output.

### 6.9 Events and seasonality → T7, RQ-35
- Event calendars contain only **scheduled** events fixed in advance (central-bank meetings, CPI, payrolls). Volume- or return-selected "events" select on the outcome and stay descriptive.
- Calendar-month seasonality has n ≈ number of years per month: low power, descriptive with CIs.
