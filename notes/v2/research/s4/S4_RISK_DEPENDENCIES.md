# S4.13 — Risk and covariance dependency inventory

**Document status:** DRAFT (S4.13; inventory only — nothing is selected, calibrated or implemented here) · **Prepared:** 2026-10-19 · **Corrected:** 2026-10-24, after the owner-requested read-only review of 2026-10-20 (corrections 1–21 authorised by the owner; changes listed in §10) · **Basis:** S4_PLAN §G (step S4.13: "Per PC method: required risk representation … estimator candidates registered only … selection stays with S10"); `S4_METHODS_OUTLINE.md` §2 (S4.13); the method records of S4.5–S4.12; decisions S412-D3, S412-D5, S412-D6 and EQ-SCN-1; `06_MODEL_ELIGIBILITY_GOVERNANCE.md` §3–§4; `07_POLICY_STATEMENT_GOVERNANCE.md` §4–§5; `S4_TAXONOMY.md` (M.RISK, A.RISK); `S4_RISK_MODEL_CHOICE.md`; RQ-14 · **Checker:** `verification/s413_risk_dependencies.py` · **Feeds:** S4.16, S4.20, S5, S6, S7, S10 (RQ-14).

**Status of ADR-0027.** ADR-0027 is **PROPOSED**, not accepted. Its D3 (one authoritative risk model per problem: universe × horizon × risk object; r3, Option A chosen by the owner on 2026-10-08) and D5 (declared units) are used here only as a provisional design input. Every requirement that depends on them is marked `[provisional: …]`. Nothing here promotes ADR-0027.

**What this document is.** A typed map from every method record in S4.5–S4.12 to the risk inputs it needs, the shared risk objects those inputs can come from, and the candidates registered to build those objects or to compute a method's risk measure from them. **What it is not.** It does not choose an estimator, set a threshold, calibrate anything, change a method specification or revise an earlier decision. A complete inventory, and a passing checker, are not evidence that the methodological choices are right.

## 0. Summary

1. **Coverage.** 28 method records (21 PC methods: A1–A5, B1–B7, C1–C5, D1–D4; SIG-1, SIG-2 and their variants R, F, F, T; the strategy-level scaling EQ-SIG-4), in 32 input rows, plus 6 cross-cutting consumers. PC-E1/E2 (S4.14) and the CRO diagnostics (S4.16) are out of scope.
2. **Eight shared risk objects** (§2): Σ for the strategic problem (RO-1) and for a security sleeve (RO-2); the short-horizon risk model (RO-3); demeaned and μ-centred scenario sets (RO-4, RO-5); return paths (RO-6); co-moments for the modified-CVaR candidate (RO-7); a factor model with regression statistics (RO-8). Objects are kept separate wherever universe, horizon, location or basis differ.
3. **24 candidates** (§5), typed by kind: 7 estimators, 2 conditional (estimator or transformation, depending on how the shrinkage intensity is set), 5 transformations of an existing estimate, 4 compositions, 3 scenario or path generators, 2 data-preparation steps and 1 risk-measure estimator (MEASURE: modified CVaR, required by S412-D3). All have status `candidate`; none is selected.
4. **No numerical eligibility threshold exists** in the repository (06 §4; RQ-14), so none is recorded. The one hard minimum is mathematical: a sample covariance from T observations has rank ≤ T − 1, so it is singular when T ≤ N (06 §4; verified in the checker).
5. **Findings** (§6). The most important:
   - **F-1 (owner clarification needed):** EQ-SCN-1 follows Grinold's eq. (10), a total-return scenario, so its μ-free scenarios keep the cash rate r_F in the location; S412-D6 calls them "demeaned", and the S4.12a results for D3 hold on zero-location scenarios. With r_F in the location, **ERC-CVaR exists only if the long-only minimum CVaR on zero-location scenarios exceeds the period cash return** (§6.1); otherwise it has no solution, and where it exists its weights move. D1 V0 is unaffected. Recorded as unresolved; S412-D6 and EQ-SCN-1 are not revised here.
   - **F-2:** through EQ-SPLIT-1 every PC candidate, including 1/N and market cap, depends on RO-1 for σ(p).
   - **F-3, F-4, F-5:** PC-B4's simulation length T is undefined for non-sample estimators; PC-B3 needs a regression-form factor model; sleeves need look-through risk (R-7).
   - **F-11:** portfolios combining asset-class units and individual securities need joint or hierarchical risk across RO-1 and RO-2; no object exists for it (unresolved, S5/S10).
6. **Status of the map.** In 30 of the 32 input rows the input type and object come directly from a method record; PC-A5's σ* problem mapping is an assumption (labelled `ASSUMED`), and PC-D4's security-level route is an option its record mentions, not a requirement. Every shared object is provisional (ADR-0027) and unresolved in at least its thresholds or data (S5, S6, S10), so every row that uses one is `unresolved` overall. That is the expected state before S10.

## 1. Conventions

### 1.1 Status markers and evidence tags

Every requirement cell carries at least one **status marker**:

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[established: <basis>]` | Fixed by an accepted ADR (0001–0026), an owner decision (S4x-Dy, OB-x), a governance document (00–10), a reviewed or done S4 method record, an equation-register entry, an invariant or rule, a failure code in a record, or a verified derivation. ADR-0027 is never a basis for this marker |
| `[provisional: <basis>]` | Taken from a PROPOSED decision (ADR-0027) or a design input that no decision has fixed |
| `[unresolved → <stage or RQ>]` | Not known or not decided; the destination is where it will be settled |
| `[object: RO-n]` | The requirement is the shared object's (§2), whose own markers apply |

The **Binding** column of each row is the weakest status among the row's markers and the objects it uses (unresolved < provisional < established). The checker recomputes it.

**Evidence tags** follow 02 §A: `VERIFIED-SOURCE` (a repository record or a primary source; "(abstract)" limits the scope to the abstract), `VERIFIED-DERIVATION`, `VERIFIED-SECONDARY` (handoff §2.2 claim label: abstract or secondary account only), `ESTABLISHED`, `ASSUMED`, `OPEN`. A row tagged `ASSUMED` or `OPEN` cannot be `established`.

### 1.2 Risk-input types

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| COV | Covariance matrix Σ (N × N); volatilities and correlations are views of it, not separate estimates |
| VOL | A volatility input: σ_i, σ(p) of a portfolio, σ̂_t, or a residual volatility; may be a view of a COV object, or of a FACTOR object for residual volatility |
| SCN | Scenario set: q × N returns with probabilities π and a scenario horizon Δt |
| PATH | Return paths: T × N returns in time order |
| COMOM | Co-moment tensors M3 (N × N²) and M4 (N × N³) |
| FACTOR | Factor model (loadings, factor covariance, residual variances), with regression statistics where a method needs them |
| INTERNAL | Estimation inside a method or signal definition, not an A.RISK object (ADR-0027 D3 point 3 calls this method-internal regularisation; provisional) |
| NONE | No risk input |

Method rows reference risk objects, never estimators. Estimators appear only in §5.

## 5. Candidates (registered, not selected)

**Kinds.**
- **ESTIMATOR** builds an object from the return observations. Ledoit–Wolf linear shrinkage is an estimator: it combines the sample covariance with a structured target, and its optimal intensity is estimated from the observations, not from the covariance matrix alone (LW 2003 Lemma 1, PDF p. 10; LW 2004 Lemma 3.3, PDF p. 15).
- **TRANSFORMATION** acts on an existing estimate (plus declared parameters such as N/T) without re-reading the observations.
- **ESTIMATOR / TRANSFORMATION (conditional on …)** marks a candidate whose kind depends on a specification not yet fixed (here, how the shrinkage intensity is set).
- **COMPOSITION** combines estimates of different parts (volatility, correlation, dynamics).
- **GENERATOR** produces scenario or path sets.
- **DATA-PREP** treats return data before any estimator.
- **MEASURE** computes a method's risk measure from a risk object and builds no object; its Objects column names the object it reads, and it never appears in an object's "Constructed by" list.

Candidates can be combined where the Relationships column says so; they are not mutually exclusive alternatives. Selection is S10's, per problem, under the S7 protocol (S4_RISK_MODEL_CHOICE §3, design input). Eligibility fields follow the 06 §3 dimensional contract (minimum N, minimum T_eff, range of q = N/T_eff, minimum N for asymptotic validity) plus the S4.13 plan's eligibility dimensions, constraint regime and horizon (ADR-0027 D3, provisional).

### 6.1 F-1 in detail: the scenario return basis

**Definitions (verified).**
- Grinold, *Advances* ch. 32, eq. (10), p. 671: with probability π_s the **total** return on asset n is r_F + μ_nΔt + σ_n z_{n,s}√Δt, where r_F is the total return on a risk-free investment over [0, Δt], μ_n the annual expected excess return, σ_n the annual volatility, and z is built from asset n's excess-return history, demeaned and standardised.
- EQ-SCN-1 reproduces this form. With μ = 0 for μ-free uses, its scenarios therefore have mean r_F, not zero.
- S4.6 §4: μ and Σ are in excess of the numéraire cash rate. S412-D6: μ-free D1 V0 and D3 use "demeaned" scenarios; its rationale includes making the normal limits (GMV, ERC) hold.
- The S4.12a verification used zero location: `s412_cvar_ru.py` recentres with `target_mu + zs*sd` and no r_F; `s412_drawdown_tail.py` tests D3 with μ = 0 and demeans its scenario set.

**D3 as specified** (S4.12a record; S412-D3). CVaR at β = 0.95 of the loss −y′w on scenarios (EQ-NT-1); Euler contributions C_i = w_i·E[−r_i | tail]; equal shares solved by the convex log-barrier y* = argmin CVaR(y) − (1/N)Σ ln y_i, with w = y*/1′y*; long-only and fully invested by construction, bounds by post-processing. S412-D3's rationale says the convex form removes the existence failure; BCP-4 names cash in a total-return CVaR as the failure case.

**Exact existence condition** (`VERIFIED-DERIVATION`; reproduced in the checker).
1. CVaR(y; Y + c·1) = CVaR(y; Y) − c·1′y for y ≥ 0 (translation equivariance and positive homogeneity).
2. If CVaR(y₀) < 0 for some y₀ ≥ 0, the barrier objective tends to −∞ along t·y₀, so it has no minimiser; an equal-share point with positive CVaR, rescaled to CVaR = 1, would be a stationary point of this convex function, hence a minimiser, so none exists. If CVaR(y) > 0 for every nonzero y ≥ 0, the objective is coercive and its minimiser is unique (the S4.12a record's existence statement).
3. Hence, with a common per-period cash return r in the location, **ERC-CVaR exists if and only if the long-only minimum CVaR on zero-location scenarios (D1 V0's optimum) exceeds r.**

**Special cases.**
- **Normal returns:** CVaR⁰(w) = c₂·σ(w)·√Δt with c₂ = φ(z_β)/(1 − β) = 2.0627 at β = 0.95, so ERC-CVaR exists if and only if the long-only minimum-variance volatility exceeds r_F√Δt/c₂, with r_F annualised: 1.94% at an annual horizon and r_F = 4%, 0.56% monthly, rising with √Δt.
- **Sufficient single-asset bounds:** under normality (BCP-4), any asset with σ_i ≤ r_F√Δt/c₂; for any distribution (Cauchy–Schwarz), any asset with σ_i ≤ r_F·√Δt·√(1 − β), 0.89% annually. They are **not necessary**: with volatilities 16%, 2.5% and 2.5% (uncorrelated) no asset is below 1.94%, yet the long-only minimum-variance volatility is 1.76% and no solution exists.

**Reproduction** (checker; S4.12a's Gaussian CVaR and the decided barrier form):

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Volatilities 16%, 6%, 1.5%; correlations 0.1, 0, 0.2; annual; r_F in the location | No solution (long-only minimum-variance volatility 1.49% < 1.94%; the barrier objective diverges) |
| Same, zero location | (0.073, 0.178, 0.749), equal to volatility-ERC |
| Same, monthly | Solution exists under both locations: (0.073, 0.178, 0.749) → (0.056, 0.138, 0.806) |
| 16%, 2.5%, 2.5%, uncorrelated, annual | No solution, although no asset is below 1.94% |
| 16%, 3%, 3%, uncorrelated, annual | Solution exists (minimum-variance volatility 2.10%) |
| Fat-tailed scenario CVaR (seeded t(4) scenarios with a crash jump) | Annual: minimum zero-location CVaR 3.37% < 4%, no solution. Monthly: 0.97% > 0.33%, solution exists; weights (0.072, 0.172, 0.756) → (0.056, 0.133, 0.811) |
| D1 V0 (min-CVaR LP), with and without the shift | Identical weights; CVaR shifts by exactly −r_FΔt |
