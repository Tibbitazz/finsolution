## 1. Paper-by-paper review

### P1 — LLM agents in experimental asset markets (v3) · **PARTIAL**

**Design.**
- Smith–Suchanek–Williams-type market: one risky asset; dividend 0.4 or 1.0 with equal probability; 5% interest on cash; constant fundamental value 14; 30 trading rounds after 3 practice rounds; call auction.
- Six API models: Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o, GPT-3.5, Grok-2, Mistral-Large, Gemini 1.5 Pro.
- Single-model markets of 20 instances each; three mixed ("battle royale") markets with 4 agents per model.
- Benchmark: 19 human markets.
- Agents keep plan and insight memory files that they update every round.

**Findings (v3).**
- Claude 3.5 Sonnet and GPT-4o trade at the fundamental value (mean squared deviation 0.54 and 0.79, against 429.8 for humans).
- GPT-3.5 and Grok-2 form bubbles. Gemini 1.5 Pro shows a reverse pattern.
- A classifier identifies which model wrote a strategy text with > 99% accuracy.
- Strategy text is fundamental-oriented for LLMs (63%) and "buy low, sell high" for humans (89%).
- Forecast-rationality tests (Table 4: unbiasedness, error autocorrelation, error ⟂ forecast) are passed at rates differing widely across models.
- The authors advise caution in replacing human subjects with off-the-shelf agents.

**Strongest objection.**
- Three independent markets per condition; no market-level inference on the price-deviation metrics.
- 2024-vintage models; a stylised single-asset market. Transfer to strategic allocation is by analogy only.

**What we take.**
1. **Instances of one model behave alike, and models differ systematically** (fingerprinting > 99%). Model choice is a first-order design variable, and same-model agents are not independent voters (RQ-56).
2. The forecast-rationality battery serves as a **process signal** for agent learning and verifiability (RQ-55, RQ-22).
3. A working example of per-agent persistent memory (RQ-22 design).

### P2 — Liang: before vs after the knowledge cutoff (GPT-4) · **PARTIAL**

**Design.**
- GPT-4, knowledge cutoff 30 Sep 2021. Forecast errors compared before and after the cutoff over Jan 2021 – Dec 2023: 9 pre-cutoff months, 27 after.
- Three tasks: daily index levels, monthly stock prices (S&P 500 and Russell 2000 members), quarterly EPS.
- Controls: size, B/M, past returns, volatility, analyst coverage, institutional ownership. Calendar-month fixed effects (firm fixed effects in the panels); standard errors clustered by year-month (p. 16).

**Findings** (pp. 1, 5–6, 18):

| Task | Absolute errors before the cutoff vs after |
|---|---|
| Daily index | ≈ 18% lower (S&P 500 ≈ 65%; Russell 2000 not significant) |
| Monthly stock prices | ≈ 16% lower; S&P 500 22%, Russell 15%, low volatility 25%, high volatility 12% |
| Quarterly EPS, press-release text | ≈ 11% lower |
| Quarterly EPS, two lagged EPS values only | ≈ 12% lower |

- The GPT–analyst accuracy gap narrows by up to ≈ 24% (high volatility) and ≈ 34% (high SUE) in the pre-cutoff period.

**Strongest objection.**
- Identification rests on 9 pre-cutoff months against a 2022–23 period with a different macro regime. Volatility and return controls address this only in part.
- Inference uses about 36 year-month clusters.
- One model.

**What we take.**
- The **direction**: pre-cutoff accuracy is inflated, and context-poor prompts do not remove it.
- The **design lesson:** compare model versions on the same target dates (§2.5) rather than one model across calendar periods.

### P3 — Gao, Jiang & Yan: Lookahead Propensity (LAP) · **FITS**

**Design.**
- Date-only recall query: entity and target date only; answer up / down / unknown.
- **LAP = P(up) + P(down)** from token probabilities at the answer position.
- Detection regression: outcome on the forecast signal × LAP. A positive interaction before the cutoff indicates contamination; the same regression after the cutoff is the placebo.
- Llama-3.3-70B, cutoff Dec 2023.

**Findings.**
- **Mean LAP by year** (headline application, Fig. p. 32):
  - between 0.18 and 0.88 over 2012–2022;
  - **0.016 in 2023**, the final pre-cutoff year;
  - **0.000 in 2024**.
  - In the earnings-call application LAP is far lower (≤ 0.05 in every year; essentially 0 after the cutoff).
- The recall signal alone predicts next-day returns (t = 3.53, p. 19).
- Signal × LAP is significant (δ̂ = 0.162, t = 3.64; an LAP of one standard deviation raises the signal's effect by about 32%; p. 20).
- Post-cutoff placebo: t = 1.06 (p. 22).

**Procedure (P3 p. 51; P3-P):**
1. Generate the production forecast exactly as deployed, at temperature 0. Parse rate ≥ 95%.
2. One recall query per (entity, target date), with no text; max 1 token; top-20 log-probabilities; raw, not renormalised, probabilities.
   - Fallback when log-probabilities are unavailable: ≥ 25 samples at temperature 1, labelled "approximated".
3. **Validation:** outcome on (P(up) − P(down)) with entity and period fixed effects, clustered by entity. Report pooled results and high/low-LAP halves.
4. **Detection:** before the cutoff, regress the outcome on the signal, LAP and signal × LAP. Test β₃ > 0 one-sided.
5. **Placebo:** repeat after the cutoff. It passes if the one-sided p-value is > 0.10.
- **Remedy if contamination is detected:** restrict backtests to post-cutoff data. Prompt-level fixes must be re-tested.

**Limits.**
- It detects contamination (its sign); it does not measure its size.
- It assumes monotonicity: higher recall means more leakage.
- It needs token probabilities or repeated sampling.
- It needs a panel with enough entities. A single entity drops entity fixed effects and needs Newey–West errors; about 20 or more clusters are needed.

**What we take.** The standard contamination test for any agent output that is evaluated on history (S7). Its requirements become DR-10.

### P4 — Didisheim, Fraschini & Somoza: "pure look-ahead bias" · **FITS**

**Design.**
- Ask GPT-4.1, 4.1-mini and 4.1-nano for a firm's or index's return on a date, giving the name, ticker and date only.
- **q-trimming:** q is the minimum share of observations that must be removed before a long–short portfolio built on the recalled returns has a Sharpe ratio below 0.2.

**Findings.**
- **Stock-level recall** (correlation of recalled with actual returns): 0.05 daily, 0.20 monthly, 0.43 yearly. The largest market-cap decile reaches 0.24, 0.45 and 0.84 (Table 1, p. 3).
  - q = 3.0%, 17.7% and 39.5% (daily, monthly, yearly).
- **Indexes:** S&P 500 monthly correlation 1.00, with the sign right 98% of the time; MSCI World 0.97; Euro Stoxx 50 0.96 (Table 3, p. 5).
- **Model size:** smaller models memorise far less. GPT-4.1-nano shows almost none daily.
- **Context:** adding the same-day S&P 500 return to the prompt raises daily q from 3.0% to 21.4% (Table 4).
- **Rewording:** for correctly recalled observations, ten reworded prompts give nearly identical outputs; for incorrectly recalled ones, outputs vary more (App. A, 558 monthly index observations).
- **Timing:** performance is concentrated in the more recent part of the extended sample (p. 3).

**Strongest objection.** One model family and one vendor; recall is measured on a single prompt design. The peer-reviewed format is short, so robustness lives in the appendices.

**What we take.**
- q-trimming as a diagnostic.
- Rewording dispersion as a detector that **needs no log-probabilities**.
- The ordering of contamination: indexes > large caps > small caps; yearly > monthly > daily; larger models > smaller.

## 2. Synthesis for the framework

### 2.1 Correction: direction of the bias
- **What we said before:** our records said the direction of LLM look-ahead bias is "not known in advance" (from Glasserman & Lin 2023).
- **Why it was too broad:** G&L compared **named vs anonymised** inputs in-sample. That comparison does not identify pre- vs post-cutoff contamination.
- **What the new evidence shows:** with cutoff-based designs, P2–P4 consistently find **inflated** in-sample accuracy.
- **Default assumption from now on (`ASSUMED`, evidence-backed):** historical agent results are **overstated** unless a diagnostic shows otherwise.
- **Limit:** the size is task-dependent and can be small (P3's earnings-call LAP ≤ 0.05), so it is measured per task, never assumed.

### 2.2 Where contamination concentrates
- **More contamination:** indexes and aggregates; large caps; longer horizons; larger models; richer context.
- **Not monotone in calendar time.** P3's final pre-cutoff year has low LAP (0.016), consistent with sparse training coverage just before the cutoff. P4 finds more recall in recent decades. The months just before a cutoff are therefore *less* contaminated, but **not clean**.

### 2.3 Consequence for this engine (`INFERENCE`)
- ANG's agents judge **asset-class indexes over monthly to yearly horizons**, the region where P4 measures near-total recall (S&P 500 monthly 1.00).
- So historical evaluation of AC/CMA, macro and CIO judgements carries **essentially no evidential weight** about skill. This strengthens R2 (deterministic-only historical evaluation), DR-5 (mode stamp) and forward testing (RQ-26).
- Deterministic layers (Sørensen scores, optimisers, covariance) are unaffected.
- **Correction to `S4_INPUTS_2026-10-07.md` §3.6:** per-company agents are the worst case for *masking* (the identity cannot be hidden). For *outcome recall*, the worst case is index-level agents, i.e. our asset-class agents.

### 2.4 Learning hygiene (owner priority; RQ-22)
- **Clean-window rule [AD, candidate]:** an outcome counts as a learning or evaluation signal for a forecast only if the target date is after the **training cutoff of the model that produced the forecast**.
- **A model upgrade resets the window.** Earlier "forward" periods become in-sample for the new model, so its record restarts.
- **Upgrade trade-off:** each upgrade restarts the learning record that S17 promotion decisions rest on. This must be weighed in model-upgrade decisions.
- Requires DR-9.

### 2.6 Homogeneity and correlated errors (RQ-56)
- P1 adds behavioural evidence to Kim et al. (2025):
  - same-model instances converge;
  - models are distinguishable (> 99% fingerprinting).
- P4 adds that larger models memorise more.
- **Model choice therefore trades capability against contamination against error correlation.** Each is measured (M5, M6), not assumed.
- Mitigation M5 (model heterogeneity) gains a contamination dimension: models with different cutoffs also differ in what they remember.
